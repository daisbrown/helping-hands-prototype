# Helping Hands — early project brief

Latricia's personal shopping and errand service serves seniors and people with disabilities in Kings Mountain and surrounding areas. Her flyer emphasizes that calling and texting remain available and an app is optional. The flyer lists groceries, medicine pickup, heavy items, and late-night or emergency errands; availability and operating details need confirmation.

## Direction

- Draw on the warmth and personality of the Beets Meets Eats prototype while honoring Latricia's pink, heart-centered flyer and keeping the interface particularly easy to use.
- Give each service a clear, direct entry point with readable type, large controls, simple steps, and an always-visible help option.
- Explain costs before confirmation and keep family members and caregivers welcome to assist.
- Use only illustrative prices and sample workflows until Latricia confirms actual details. Clearly label a prototype; do not imply that a request was sent or a payment processed when it was not.
- The flyer lists 704-438-5683; confirm its use before enabling call or text links.

## Open decisions

- First deliverable: clickable prototype or informational website; photography versus illustration; permission to use the flyer phone number.
- Confirm services, coverage, hours, service fees, pricing, scheduling, and how requests reach Latricia.
- Decide whether customers or caregivers should be able to request a service without an account.
- Card scanning, identity documents, payment cards, FSA eligibility and prescription information need a separate privacy, security, payment-provider and operational review before any real data is collected. The demo should not request or store real sensitive information.
- Hosting and ongoing costs depend on whether this remains a static demo or becomes a real ordering service.
